Lab Coat Initiative

Ongoing

A secondhand lab coat rental program that gives students affordable access to lab coats while keeping usable coats out of the landfill. Instead of buying new, students rent a coat for the term and return it to be cleaned and reused.

PET Extrusion

Ongoing

Turning used PET plastic bottles into 3D printer filament. The project cuts bottles into strips and extrudes them into usable filament, closing the loop on plastic waste and producing low-cost material for campus 3D printing.

Compost Bioreactor

Ongoing

A pilot composting system that turns spent coffee grounds from the campus Head Rest Cafe into usable compost for UNB landscaping. The enclosed, aerated design controls odour and leachate so it can run on campus, and it collects temperature and moisture data to guide a future scale-up decision.

Offshore Wind Challenge Team

Ongoing

A student team competing in the international Floating Wind Challenge, where engineering students design a floating platform for an offshore wind turbine. Our team has secured initial funding and is researching materials and platform concepts as it works toward an abstract, a built model, and the international competition in 2027.

Pedal Power

Completed

A human-powered bicycle generator built from salvaged parts. A standard bike drives a junkyard car alternator through a 16.5:1 belt step-up, reaching roughly 1,485 RPM at normal pedalling cadence. The prototype hit a peak charging voltage of 14.41 V at around 100 RPM and cost CAD $221 to build, about 6 to 8 times less than comparable commercial pedal generators.

Nitrile Gloves Upcycling

Completed

Turning used nitrile gloves, a major source of lab waste, into useful products instead of sending them to landfill or incineration. Uncontaminated gloves are collected, shredded, and hot-pressed into durable items like lab mats. A single first-year biology course yielded about 1,500 gloves per term, and on-site processing on campus proved feasible.
